## Section 1: Arson, Fire Debris & Explosives

### 1.1 Chemistry of Fire & Combustion Dynamics

Fire or combustion is a rapid, self-sustaining exothermic chemical oxidation reaction in which a fuel combines with oxygen in the presence of heat, liberating heat, light, and reaction products [Saferstein, Ch. 12; Evans-Nguyen, Ch. 1].

```
                     The Fire Tetrahedron
                              
                           ▲ HEAT
                          ╱ ╲
                         ╱   ╲
                        ╱     ╲
                       ╱       ╲
     OXYGEN (OXIDIZER) ───────── FUEL (REDUCANT)
                           │
                CHAIN REACTION (UNINHIBITED)
```

#### The Fire Triangle vs. Fire Tetrahedron:
* **Fire Triangle:** The classic model requiring three essential components:
  1. **Fuel:** Any combustible material (solid, liquid, or gas).
  2. **Oxidizer:** Typically atmospheric oxygen ($21\% \text{ O}_2$), though chemical oxidizers ($\text{KClO}_3, \text{KNO}_3$) can supply oxygen.
  3. **Heat / Energy:** Ignition source sufficient to raise the fuel temperature above its ignition point.
* **Fire Tetrahedron:** Adds a fourth critical dimension — the **uninhibited chemical chain reaction** of free radicals (e.g., $\text{H}^\bullet, \text{OH}^\bullet, \text{O}^\bullet$) in the gas phase. Halon-based fire suppressants work specifically by scavenging these free radicals [Evans-Nguyen, Ch. 1].

#### Key Thermal & Physical Parameters:
* **Oxidation:** The chemical combination of oxygen with other substances to produce new compounds.
* **Exothermic Reaction:** A chemical reaction that releases thermal energy (heat). Combustion reactions are strongly exothermic [Saferstein, Ch. 12].
* **Energy of Activation:** The minimum energy required to initiate a chemical reaction (breaking existing bonds to form new ones).
* **Flash Point:** The lowest temperature at which a liquid fuel produces enough vapor to form an ignitable mixture with air near the surface. (e.g., Gasoline: $-43^\circ\text{C}$; Kerosene: $+38^\circ\text{C}$ to $+72^\circ\text{C}$).
* **Fire Point / Flame Point:** The temperature at which liquid fuel produces sufficient vapors to sustain continuous burning once ignited (typically a few degrees above the flash point).
* **Ignition Temperature:** The minimum temperature to which a fuel-air mixture must be heated to initiate self-sustained combustion without an external spark or flame.
* **Pyrolysis:** The thermal decomposition of solid organic matter (e.g., wood, paper, plastics) by heat in the absence/presence of oxygen, producing gaseous fuel vapors capable of burning [Saferstein, Ch. 12].
* **Flammability Limits (LEL / UEL):** The concentration range of fuel vapor in air that will burn or explode if ignited.
  * *Lower Explosive Limit (LEL):* Minimum concentration of fuel vapor below which the mixture is too "lean" to burn.
  * *Upper Explosive Limit (UEL):* Maximum concentration of fuel vapor above which the mixture is too "rich" to burn.

#### Smoke Staining & V-Pattern Interpretation at Fire Scenes:
* **V-Pattern:** As a fire burns upward and outward, soot, smoke, and heat create a V-shaped pattern on vertical walls or surfaces. The point or vertex of the "V" points directly down toward the **origin of the fire** [Saferstein, Ch. 12].
* **Smoke & Soot Staining:** Heavy soot deposits indicate incomplete combustion of petroleum-based accelerants or synthetic polymers. Clean burn areas (where intense heat has burned away soot) often indicate the origin of maximum heat [Evans-Nguyen, Ch. 2].
* **Puddle / Pour Patterns:** Irregular, pooling burn patterns on floors with sharp margins suggest the use of a liquid accelerant (gasoline, kerosene, diesel).

### 1.2 Analysis of Fire Debris & Ignitable Liquids

#### Recovery & Packaging of Arson Evidence:
* **Packaging Integrity:** Fire debris suspected of containing ignitable liquid residues (ILRs) must be packaged immediately in **airtight, non-reactive containers** to prevent volatile vapors from escaping [Saferstein, Ch. 12].
* **Approved Containers:** Clean, unused paint cans with friction-fit lids, or specialized heat-sealed nylon/polyester bags.
* **Prohibited Containers:** Standard polyethylene plastic bags (volatile hydrocarbons dissolve/penetrate plastic films) and paper bags (vapor loss).

```
                 Fire Debris Extraction & Analysis Workflow
                                     │
         ┌───────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                                       ▼
  PASSIVE HEADSPACE EXTRACTION                             SOLID-PHASE MICROEXTRACTION
  (Charcoal Strip In Can)                                  (SPME Fiber In Headspace)
         │                                                       │
         ▼                                                       ▼
  Solvent Elution (Carbon Disulfide CS2)                   Thermal Desorption In Injector
         │                                                       │
         └───────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────┘
                                     ▼
                      GAS CHROMATOGRAPHY-MASS SPECTROMETRY
                           (GC-MS Analysis ASTM E1618)
```

#### Extraction Techniques for Ignitable Liquid Residues (ILRs):
1. **Direct Headspace Sampling:** Sampling vapor directly above debris inside a warmed, sealed container using a gas-tight syringe. Simple but low sensitivity for heavy distillates [Evans-Nguyen, Ch. 3].
2. **Passive Headspace Concentration (Charcoal Strip):** An activated charcoal strip is suspended inside the sealed container and heated to $60^\circ\text{C}-80^\circ\text{C}$ for 2–16 hours. Volatile hydrocarbons adsorb onto the charcoal. The strip is removed and eluted with carbon disulfide ($\text{CS}_2$) [Saferstein, Ch. 12; Evans-Nguyen, Ch. 3].
3. **Solid-Phase Microextraction (SPME):** A fused-silica fiber coated with a polymeric stationary phase (e.g., PDMS) is inserted into the container headspace to absorb vapors, then thermally desorbed directly into the GC injection port [Evans-Nguyen, Ch. 3].

#### GC-MS Analysis & ASTM E1618 Classification:
Fire debris extracts are analyzed using **Gas Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry (GC-MS)**. Standard ASTM E1618 categorizes ignitable liquids into distinct classes based on chromatographic boiling-point profiles and target ion monitoring ($m/z$ ratios):
* *Light Petroleum Distillates (LPD):* $C_4-C_9$ (e.g., pocket lighter fluid, camping fuel).
* *Medium Petroleum Distillates (MPD):* $C_8-C_{13}$ (e.g., paint thinners, mineral spirits).
* *Heavy Petroleum Distillates (HPD):* $C_9-C_{22+}$ (e.g., diesel fuel, kerosene, heating oil).
* *Gasoline:* Characterized by aromatic hydrocarbons (toluene, ethylbenzene, xylenes, $C_3$-alkylbenzenes) and indanes [Saferstein, Ch. 12].

### 1.3 Classification of Explosives

An explosive is a chemical compound or mixture that, upon application of heat, impact, friction, or shock, undergoes an extremely rapid chemical decomposition, releasing massive volumes of heated gases and shock pressure waves [Saferstein, Ch. 12; Evans-Nguyen, Ch. 4].

```
                          Master Explosives Classification
                                         │
             ┌───────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────┐
             ▼                                                       ▼
      LOW EXPLOSIVES                                          HIGH EXPLOSIVES
   (Deflagration < 1000 m/s)                               (Detonation 1000–9000 m/s)
   ├─ Black Powder (KNO3+S+C)                                 │
   └─ Smokeless Powder (NC/NG)              ┌─────────────────┴─────────────────┐
                                            ▼                                   ▼
                                   PRIMARY HIGH EXPLOSIVES             SECONDARY HIGH EXPLOSIVES
                                   (Ultra-sensitive to impact/shock)   (Insensitive; requires booster)
                                   ├─ Lead Azide                       ├─ TNT, RDX, PETN, HMX
                                   ├─ Fulminate of Mercury             └─ Commercial Blasting Agents
                                   └─ DDNP, Lead Styphnate                 (ANFO, Slurries, Dynamites)
```

#### Deflagration vs. Detonation:
* **Deflagration:** A rapid chemical burning process where the reaction wave propagates through the material at **subsonic speeds** (less than the speed of sound in the unreacted material, $<1000\text{ m/s}$). Characteristic of low explosives [Saferstein, Ch. 12].
* **Detonation:** An instantaneous chemical reaction where a high-pressure shock wave propagates through the explosive at **supersonic speeds** ($1000\text{ m/s}$ to $9000\text{ m/s}$), causing sudden shattering effects (brisance). Characteristic of high explosives [Saferstein, Ch. 12].

#### Comprehensive Classification Matrix:

| Category | Reaction Mode & Speed | Physical/Chemical Characteristics | Key Examples | Primary Forensic Utility / Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Low Explosives** | **Deflagration** ($<1000\text{ m/s}$). Requires confinement to explode. | Burning rate controlled by grain size and surface area. Produces high gas volumes. | • **Black Powder:** Potassium nitrate ($75\%$), Charcoal ($15\%$), Sulfur ($10\%$).<br>• **Smokeless Powder:** Single-base (Nitrocellulose) or Double-base (Nitrocellulose + Nitroglycerin). | Pipe bombs, propellant cartridges, fireworks, safety fuses [Saferstein, Ch. 12]. |

Ultra-sensitive. | Extremely sensitive to heat, friction, impact, and electrostatic discharge. | • **Lead Azide** $[\text{Pb(N}_3)_2]$<br>• **Fulminate of Mercury** $[\text{Hg(CNO)}_2]$<br>• **Lead Styphnate**<br>• **DDNP** (Diazodinitrophenol) | Used in blasting caps, primers, and detonators to initiate secondary explosives [Saferstein, Ch. 12]. |

### 1.4 Field & Laboratory Detection Procedures for Explosives

#### Field Detection & Concealed Explosives Screening:
1. **K-9 Explosive Detection Dogs (EDDs):** Highly sensitive biological sensors trained to detect volatile headspace vapors of nitro-organic compounds (TNT, RDX, PETN, TATP).
2. **Ion Mobility Spectrometry (IMS):** Samples are wiped or vapor-sampled, ionized (typically using a $^{63}\text{Ni}$ radioactive beta source), and driven down a drift tube against an opposing gas flow under an electric field. Compounds are identified by their characteristic **drift time / ion mobility** [Saferstein, Ch. 12; Evans-Nguyen, Ch. 5].
3. **Colorimetric Spot Tests (Presumptive Field Testing):**
  * *Griess Reagent:* Tests for nitrites/nitrates. Yields a deep pink/red azo dye.
  * *Diphenylamine Test:* Tests for nitramines and nitrate esters. Yields an intense blue color in concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$.
  * *KOH / Acetone Test (Janovsky Reaction):* Tests for polynitro-aromatics (TNT). Yields a reddish-purple Meisenheimer complex [Evans-Nguyen, Ch. 5].

#### Laboratory Instrumental Analysis Workflow:
* **Microscopic Inspection:** Examining unexploded powder grains, fuse remnants, or device debris under stereomicroscopy.
* **Chemical Spot Tests & TLC:** Initial screening for ionic and neutral organic explosives.
* **FTIR & Raman Spectroscopy:** Non-destructive identification of pure explosive crystals and inorganic oxidizer salts ($\text{KNO}_3, \text{NH}_4\text{NO}_3$).
* **GC-MS & LC-MS/MS:** Confirmatory identification and quantitation of organic high explosives (TNT, RDX, PETN, TATP) and their degradation products [Saferstein, Ch. 12].

## Section 2: Forensic Toxicology & Controlled Substances

### 2.1 Fundamentals of Controlled Substances & Narcotics

Forensic drug chemistry involves the legal, qualitative, and quantitative analysis of controlled substances as defined under the Controlled Substances Act (CSA) or international drug control conventions [Saferstein, Ch. 11].

```
                        Controlled Substance Categories
                                       │
     ┌──────────────────┬──────────────┼──────────────┬──────────────────┐
     ▼                  ▼              ▼              ▼                  ▼
  OPIATES /          STIMULANTS     DEPRESSANTS    HALLUCINOGENS      CANNABIS &
  OPIOIDS            ├─ Cocaine     ├─ Alcohol     ├─ LSD, PCP        SYNTHETICS
  ├─ Morphine        ├─ Amphetamine ├─ Barbiturates├─ Psilocybin      ├─ THC
  ├─ Heroin          └─ Meth        └─ Benzos      └─ Mescaline       └─ Fentanyl
  └─ Codeine
```

#### Major Drug Classes:
1. **Opiates & Opioids (Analgesics / Narcotics):** Depress the central nervous system (CNS), relieve pain, induce sleep, and cause physical addiction.
   * *Natural Opiates:* Morphine, Codeine (derived from the opium poppy, *Papaver somniferum*).
   * *Semi-Synthetic:* Heroin (Diacetylmorphine - prepared by acetylating morphine with acetic anhydride), Oxycodone, Hydrocodone.
   * *Synthetic Opioids:* Fentanyl (50–100$\times$ more potent than morphine), Methadone, Buprenorphine [Saferstein, Ch. 11].
2. **CNS Stimulants:** Accelerate central nervous system activity, increasing alertness, heart rate, and blood pressure.
   * *Cocaine:* Alkaloid extracted from *Erythroxylum coca*. Freebase / Crack vs. Cocaine Hydrochloride.
   * *Amphetamines & Methamphetamine:* Synthetic sympathomimetic agents causing intense euphoria and central excitation.
3. **CNS Depressants:** Depress brain activity, inducing relaxation, sedation, and sleep.
   * *Barbiturates:* Amobarbital, Secobarbital, Phenobarbital.
   * *Benzodiazepines:* Diazepam (Valium), Alprazolam (Xanax), Midazolam, Flunitrazepam (Rohypnol - "date rape" drug).
4. **Hallucinogens:** Alter perceptions, mood, thought processes, and spatial/temporal awareness.
   * *LSD (Lysergic Acid Diethylamide):* Derived from ergot fungus; extremely potent microgram-dose hallucinogen.
   * *Phencyclidine (PCP) & Ketamine:* Dissociative anesthetics.
   * *Psilocybin:* Fungal alkaloid from *Psilocybe* mushrooms.
   * *MDMA (Ecstasy):* $3,4$-Methylenedioxymethamphetamine (empathogen-stimulant).
5. **Cannabis & Emerging Synthetic NPS:**
   * *Cannabis sativa:* Active psychoactive principle is $\Delta^9$-tetrahydrocannabinol ($\Delta^9$-THC). Presumptive Duquenois-Levine test yields a violet-blue color in the chloroform layer [Saferstein, Ch. 11].
   * *Synthetic Cannabinoids (Spice/K2) & Synthetic Cathinones (Bath Salts).*

### 2.3 Analytical Toxicology Workflow & Widmark BAC Calculation

#### Two-Tiered Analytical Strategy:
1. **Screening Tests (Presumptive):** High-throughput, sensitive methods designed to rapidly eliminate negative samples and identify potential drug classes.
   * *Techniques:* Immunoassays (ELISA, EMIT, FPIA) and Thin Layer Chromatography (TLC).
2. **Confirmatory Tests (Definitive):** Highly specific, quantitative instruments that unambiguously identify the exact chemical structure and concentration.
   * *Techniques:* GC-MS, LC-MS/MS, and High-Resolution Mass Spectrometry (HRMS) [Saferstein, Ch. 11].

```
                     Toxicology Two-Tier Analytical Workflow
                                        │
                       PRESUMPTIVE IMMUNOASSAY SCREEN
                       (ELISA / EMIT / FPIA Screening)
                                        │
             ┌──────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────┐
             ▼                                                     ▼
      NEGATIVE RESULT                                       POSITIVE RESULT
    (Report Non-Detected)                                  (Requires Confirmation)
                                                                   │
                                                                   ▼
                                                     GC-MS / LC-MS/MS CONFIRMATION
                                                     (Definitive Quantitation)
```

#### Blood Alcohol Concentration (BAC) & Widmark's Formula:
In forensic alcohol analysis, Widmark's equation relates consumed alcohol dose to peak BAC and elimination kinetics [Biswas, Ch. 10; Parikh, Ch. 6]:

$$\text{BAC} = \left[ \frac{A}{P \cdot r} \right] - (\beta \cdot t)$$

* **Defined Variables & Units:**
  * $A$ = Total mass of pure ethanol absorbed ($\text{grams}$).
  * $P$ = Body mass of the subject ($\text{kilograms, kg}$).
  * $r$ = Widmark distribution factor ($\text{L/kg}$ or unitless fraction representing body water distribution: average male $r \approx 0.68$, average female $r \approx 0.55$).
  * $\beta$ = Hourly metabolic elimination rate of alcohol ($\text{g/dL per hour}$ or $\% \text{ w/v per hour}$, typical average $\beta \approx 0.015\% \text{ g/dL/hr}$).
  * $t$ = Time elapsed since the start of drinking ($\text{hours}$).

## Section 3: Forensic Serology & Biological Body Fluids

### 3.1 Forensic Characterization of Bloodstains

Blood is a complex connective tissue composed of a liquid cellular suspension (**plasma**, $55\%$) containing formed elements (**erythrocytes** [RBCs], **leukocytes** [WBCs], and **thrombocytes** [platelets], $45\%$) [Saferstein, Ch. 10; Li, Ch. 1; Forensic Serology].

```
                      Bloodstain Analysis Cascade
                                   │
                    PRESUMPTIVE COLORIMETRIC TEST
                 (Kastle-Meyer / LMG / Luminol Screen)
                                   │
             ┌─────────────────────┴─────────────────────┐
             ▼                                           ▼
      NEGATIVE RESULT                             POSITIVE RESULT
   (Not Blood / Stop)                         (Proceed To Confirmation)
                                                         │
                                                         ▼
                                             CONFIRMATORY CRYSTAL TEST
                                           (Takayama / Teichmann Test)
                                                         │
                                                         ▼
                                              SPECIES ORIGIN TESTING
                                          (ABAcard / Ouchterlony Test)
```

#### 1. Presumptive (Screening) Tests for Blood:
Presumptive tests depend on the **peroxidase-like activity** of the iron-containing **heme** group of hemoglobin, which catalyzes the oxidation of a colorless chromogenic substrate by hydrogen peroxide ($\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$) to produce a characteristic color or luminescence [Saferstein, Ch. 10; Li, Ch. 2]:

$$\text{Chromogen (Reduced/Colorless)} + \text{H}_2\text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Heme (Peroxidase Activity)}} \text{Chromogen (Oxidized/Colored)} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$

* **Kastle-Meyer (KM) Test / Phenolphthalein:** Phenolphthalin (reduced) is oxidized by heme and $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$ to phenolphthalein, producing an immediate **bright pink** color [Saferstein, Ch. 10; Li, Ch. 2]. Highly sensitive ($1:10^5$ dilution).
* **Leucomalachite Green (LMG):** Oxidized to yield a **blue-green** color [Li, Ch. 2].
* **Hemastix Strips:** Plastic strips coated with tetramethylbenzidine (TMB). Yields a **dark green/blue** color.
* **Luminol & Fluorescein (Chemiluminescence / Fluorescence):** Reacts with bloodstains to produce intense **blue-green chemiluminescence** in total darkness. Highly effective for detecting washed, diluted, or latent bloodstains at crime scenes [Saferstein, Ch. 10; Li, Ch. 2].

#### 2. Confirmatory Tests for Blood:
Confirmatory tests definitively establish the presence of blood by forming characteristic microscopic hemoglobin derivative crystals [Saferstein, Ch. 10; Li, Ch. 3; Biswas, Ch. 5]:

```
               Teichmann Rhombic Crystals vs. Takayama Feathery Crystals
               
                  ┌─────────────────┐             ┌─────────────────┐
                  │  ░░▒▒▓▓▒▒░░     │             │    \\  //        │
                  │  ░░▒▒▓▓▒▒░░     │             │     \\//         │
                  │  (Dark Brown    │             │  ====X====      │
                  │   Rhombic)      │             │     //\\         │
                  │                 │             │    //  \\        │
                  └─────────────────┘             └─────────────────┘
                   TEICHMANN HEMIN                 TAKAYAMA HEMOCHROMOGEN
```

* **Takayama Test (Hemochromogen Crystal Test):** Blood is treated with Takayama reagent (pyridine, $10\%\text{ NaOH}$, glucose, and water) and gently heated. Pyridine binds to reduced heme to form **pink, feathery, needle-like crystals of pyridine hemochromogen** [Saferstein, Ch. 10; Li, Ch. 3]. More reliable than Teichmann on aged stains.
* **Teichmann Test (Hemin Crystal Test):** Blood is heated with glacial acetic acid and sodium chloride ($\text{NaCl}$). Hemoglobin is converted into hemin chloride, forming **dark brown, rhombic, halogen-hemin crystals** [Li, Ch. 3; Biswas, Ch. 5].

#### 3. Species Origin Determination:
Determines whether human or animal blood is present:
* **ABAcard Hematrace Immunochromatographic Assay:** Membrane test using mobile anti-human hemoglobin ($\text{Hb}$) antibody-gold conjugates. A pink line at the test region ($T$) confirms **human blood** [Li, Ch. 4].
* **Ouchterlony Double Immunodiffusion:** Human antiserum and sample extract diffuse toward each other in agar gel. A visible white **precipitin line** forms where antibodies cross-link human antigens [Saferstein, Ch. 10; Li, Ch. 4].

### 3.2 Collection, Packaging & Preservation Protocols

```
                 Biological Evidence Packaging Rules
                 
             ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
             │   AIR-DRY AT ROOM TEMPERATURE           │
             │   (No heat, direct sun, or ovens)       │
             └────────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                                  │
                                  ▼
             ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
             │   BREATHABLE PAPER CONTAINERS           │
             │   (Paper bags, envelopes, boxes)        │
             └────────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                                  │
                                  ▼
             ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
             │   STRICTLY PROHIBITED: PLASTIC BAGS     │
             │   (Traps moisture -> Bacterial growth   │
             │    -> DNA degradation)                  │
             └─────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### Standard Operating Procedures:
1. **Air-Drying Requirement:** All moist or wet biological stains (blood, semen, saliva) on clothing or swabs must be thoroughly **air-dried at room temperature** in a secure, draft-free environment before packaging [Saferstein, Ch. 10]. Never use heat lamps or hair dryers.
2. **Breathable Packaging:** Package dried biological items in **breathable paper bags, envelopes, or cardboard boxes**. Plastic bags trap residual humidity, creating a warm, moist microclimate that promotes bacterial and fungal growth, rapidly destroying DNA [Saferstein, Ch. 10; Li, Ch. 1].
3. **Liquid Blood Controls:** Liquid reference blood from living subjects must be drawn into vacuum tubes containing **EDTA** (purple top) as an anticoagulant/preservative and stored at $4^\circ\text{C}$ [Saferstein, Ch. 10].

## Section 4: Bloodstain Pattern Analysis (BPA) & Crime Scene Reconstruction

### 4.1 Physics & Mechanics of Droplet Formation

Bloodstain Pattern Analysis (BPA) is the study of the shapes, sizes, locations, and distribution patterns of bloodstains to interpret the physical events that created them [Saferstein, Ch. 10; Li, Ch. 9; BPA PPT].

```
                     Physical Forces Acting on Falling Blood
                                       
                                 ▲ SURFACE TENSION
                                 │ (Pulls drop into sphere)
                          ┌──────┴──────┐
                          │  BLOOD DROP │ ──► VISCOSITY (Resists flow)
                          └──────┬──────┘
                                 │
                                 ▼ GRAVITY (Accelerates downward)
```

#### Fluid Dynamics of Blood:
* **Composition Effect:** Blood behaves as a non-Newtonian fluid with a viscosity 4 times greater than water and a surface tension of $\sim 50\text{ dynes/cm}$ [Li, Ch. 9].
* **Spherical Shape:** Surface tension continuously pulls an airborne blood droplet into a **perfect sphere** during flight. A falling blood drop will not break up spontaneously unless acted upon by an external kinetic force [Li, Ch. 9; BPA PPT].
* **Target Surface Texture:** The smoother and nonporous the target surface (e.g., glass, tile), the cleaner and smoother the bloodstain margins. Rough, porous surfaces (e.g., concrete, unpainted wood, carpet) cause edge disruption, producing extensive **scalloping, spatter, and spines** [Saferstein, Ch. 10; Li, Ch. 9].

---

### 4.2 Bloodstain Geometry & Trigonometric Calculations

#### 1. Directionality of Flight:
* As a blood drop impacts a surface at an angle, the pointed tail or satellite spatter **points in the direction of travel** [Saferstein, Ch. 10; BPA PPT].

```
                          Impact Angle Geometry
                          
           Target Surface ──────────────────────┐
                                 ╱│             │
                             L  ╱ │ W           │ Width (W)
                               ╱  │             │ ───────── = sin(θ)
                              ╱   │             │ Length (L)
                             ╱ θ  │             │
                            └─────┴─────────────┘
```

#### 2. Angle of Impact Formula:
The acute angle ($\theta$) formed between the path of a blood drop and the plane of the surface it impacts is calculated using the width-to-length ratio of the elliptical stain [Saferstein, Ch. 10; Li, Ch. 9; BPA PPT]:

$$\sin(\theta) = \frac{\text{Width } (W)}{\text{Length } (L)} \implies \theta = \arcsin\left(\frac{W}{L}\right)$$

* *Note:* Measure the width ($W$) and length ($L$) of the main stain ellipse **excluding** tail spines or satellite spatters.

#### 3. Area of Convergence vs. Area of Origin:
* **Area of Convergence (2D):** The two-dimensional point on a two-dimensional plane where lines drawn through the long axes of individual impact spatters intersect [Saferstein, Ch. 10].
* **Area of Origin (3D):** The three-dimensional point in space from which the blood drop originated. Calculated by projecting impact angles ($\theta$) upward from the 2D convergence point using right-triangle trigonometry [Saferstein, Ch. 10; Li, Ch. 9]:

$$\text{Height } (H) = D \cdot \tan(\theta)$$

where $D$ is the distance from the individual bloodstain to the 2D Area of Convergence.

```
                  Area of Convergence vs. Area of Origin
                  
                        AREA OF ORIGIN (3D Space)
                                   ▲
                                  ╱│
                                 ╱ │ Height (H) = D * tan(θ)
                                ╱  │
                               ╱   │
       AREA OF CONVERGENCE (2D) ───┼─────────── Target Surface
                                   │
                               ◄───D───► Distance to stain
```

### 4.3 Classification of Bloodstain Patterns

Bloodstain patterns are classified into three major structural categories based on the mechanism of deposition [Saferstein, Ch. 10; Li, Ch. 9; BPA PPT]:

```
                       Master BPA Classification
                                   │
     ┌─────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┐
     ▼                             ▼                             ▼
  PASSIVE STAINS                SPATTER STAINS               TRANSFER STAINS
  (Gravity alone)           (Kinetic force applied)       (Contact between surfaces)
  ├─ Drips & Drip Trails    ├─ Impact Spatter (Low/Med/High)├─ Swipe (Moving bloodied object)
  ├─ Flows & Pools          ├─ Cast-Off                   └─ Wipe (Object through wet blood)
  └─ Saturation             ├─ Arterial Spray/Gushing
                            └─ Expirated Blood
```

#### Detailed BPA Pattern Matrix:

| Pattern Category | Sub-Type / Pattern Name | Droplet Velocity & Physical Characteristics | Primary Forensic Mechanism & Cause |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Passive Stains** | **Passive Drips / Pools** | Created by gravity alone acting on liquid blood. Circular stains ($90^\circ$ impact). | Blood dripping from a static wound, bloody knife, or pooling on a floor [Saferstein, Ch. 10]. |
| **Spatter Stains** | **Low-Velocity Impact Spatter** | Droplet velocity $<1.5\text{ m/s}$; stain diameter $>4\text{ mm}$. | Produced by gravity or low-force impact (e.g., passive stepping in blood, free fall) [Saferstein, Ch. 10]. |
| **Spatter Stains** | **Medium-Velocity Impact Spatter** | Droplet velocity $1.5-7.5\text{ m/s}$; stain diameter $1-4\text{ mm}$. | Characteristic of **blunt-force trauma** (stabbing, clubbing, fist beating) [Saferstein, Ch. 10]. |
| **Spatter Stains** | **High-Velocity Impact Spatter** | Droplet velocity $\ge 30\text{ m/s}$; stain diameter $<1\text{ mm}$ (fine misting). | Characteristic of **gunshot trauma**, high-speed machinery, or explosive force [Saferstein, Ch. 10]. |
| **Spatter Stains** | **Cast-Off Pattern** | Arc-like linear trail of drops flung from a blooded object in motion. | Created when blood is flung from a bloody weapon (hammer, pipe) during backswings [Saferstein, Ch. 10]. |
| **Spatter Stains** | **Arterial Spray / Gushing** | Large spurt patterns displaying undulating crests and high volume. | Created when a major artery is severed under high systemic blood pressure [Saferstein, Ch. 10]. |
| **Spatter Stains** | **Expirated Blood Pattern** | Spatter containing **air bubbles, mucus, or saliva dilution** (pinkish). | Blood expelled from the nose, mouth, or respiratory tract under internal air pressure [Saferstein, Ch. 10]. |
| **Transfer Stains** | **Swipe Pattern** | Blood transferred from a bloodied moving surface onto a clean surface. | Moving a bloody hand or hair across a clean wall [Saferstein, Ch. 10]. |
| **Transfer Stains** | **Wipe Pattern** | Created when an object moves through a pre-existing wet bloodstain. | Attempting to clean or wipe away a pre-existing bloodstain on a floor [Saferstein, Ch. 10]. |
| **Altered Stains** | **Void Pattern / Skeletonized** | Unstained area within continuous spatter (shadowing) or dried stain perimeter. | Indicates an intermediate object/person blocked spatter or stain dried before wiping [Saferstein, Ch. 10]. |

## Section 5: Genetic Markers, DNA Profiling & Parentage Testing

### 5.1 Serological Genetic Markers & Secretor Status

Before DNA profiling, forensic individualization relied on polymorphic red cell blood groups and protein isoenzymes [Saferstein, Ch. 10; Li, Ch. 10; Parikh, Ch. 3].

#### 1. The ABO Blood Group System:
* Determined by carbohydrate antigens (A and B) on red blood cell membranes and naturally occurring antibodies (Anti-B and Anti-A) in serum [Saferstein, Ch. 10].

| Blood Group (Phenotype) | Antigens on Red Cells | Antibodies in Serum | Genotypes | Population Prevalence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A** | A Antigen | Anti-B | $AA, AO$ | $\sim 41\%$ |
| **B** | B Antigen | Anti-A | $BB, BO$ | $\sim 10\%$ |
| **AB** | Both A & B Antigens | Neither Anti-A nor Anti-B | $AB$ | $\sim 4\%$ |
| **O** | Neither A nor B (H Antigen) | Both Anti-A and Anti-B | $OO$ | $\sim 45\%$ |

#### 2. Secretor Status (FUT2 Gene):
* **Secretor Concept:** Approximately **$80\%$ of the human population** are "secretors." Secretors express ABO blood group antigens in their body fluids (saliva, semen, sweat, vaginal secretions, gastric juice) in addition to erythrocytes [Saferstein, Ch. 10; Li, Ch. 10].
* **Genetic Mechanism:** Controlled by the **FUT2 gene** (fucosyltransferase 2). Non-secretors ($20\%$) possess homozygous recessive mutations ($se/se$) and do not secrete soluble ABO antigens in saliva or semen [Li, Ch. 10].

#### 3. Typing Dried Bloodstains:
* **Absorption-Inhibition Method:** Dried stain extract is incubated with known antibodies (Anti-A). If A antigen is present in the stain, it binds (absorbs) the Anti-A antibody, inhibiting its ability to agglutinate test RBCs [Li, Ch. 10].
* **Absorption-Elution Method:** Antibodies bind to dried stain antigens; unbound antibodies are washed away. Heating ($56^\circ\text{C}$) elutes bound antibodies, which are then detected via agglutination with indicator RBCs [Li, Ch. 10].

### 5.2 Forensic DNA Profiling Workflow

Modern forensic individualization relies on short tandem repeat (STR) analysis of nuclear DNA [Saferstein, Ch. 13; Li, Ch. 11-15].

```
                       Forensic DNA Analysis Workflow
                                     │
                    DNA EXTRACTION & PURIFICATION
           (Organic / Chelex / Differential / Magnetic Beads)
                                     │
                                     ▼
                      REAL-TIME qPCR QUANTITATION
                      (TaqMan Human vs. Male Yield)
                                     │
                                     ▼
                     MULTIPLEX PCR STR AMPLIFICATION
                     (Thermal Cycling - CODIS 20 Core Loci)
                                     │
                                     ▼
                  CAPILLARY ELECTROPHORESIS & DETECTION
                  (Laser-Induced Fluorescence LIF Analysis)
                                     │
                                     ▼
                  ALLELE CALLING & EPG INTERPRETATION
```

#### 1. DNA Extraction Methods:
* **Organic Phenol-Chloroform Extraction:** Cell lysis $\rightarrow$ Phenol:Chloroform:Isoamyl alcohol ($25:24:1$) addition $\rightarrow$ Proteins dissolve in organic layer; purified DNA remains in aqueous upper phase $\rightarrow$ Ethanol precipitation [Li, Ch. 11].
* **Chelex-100 Extraction:** Chelex resin chelates polyvalent metal ions ($\text{Mg}^{2+}$), inhibiting DNase enzymes during boiling lysis [Li, Ch. 11].
* **Differential Extraction (Sexual Assault Evidence):**
  1. Mild lysis buffer (Proteinase K + SDS without DTT) lyses female vaginal epithelial cells; sperm heads remain intact $\rightarrow$ Centrifuge $\rightarrow$ Female DNA in supernatant [Saferstein, Ch. 13; Li, Ch. 11].
  2. Sperm pellet is washed and lysed using DTT (Dithiothreitol), which cleaves disulfide bonds in sperm nuclear protamines $\rightarrow$ Purified male DNA [Li, Ch. 11].

#### 2. DNA Quantitation (Real-Time qPCR):
* Crucial prior to PCR to ensure optimal DNA input ($\sim 0.5-1.0\text{ ng}$). Uses TaqMan fluorescent probes to quantify human nuclear DNA and human male Y-DNA [Li, Ch. 12].

#### 3. Short Tandem Repeat (STR) Profiling:
* **STR Definition:** Short, repeating DNA sequences ($2-6\text{ bp}$ in length) scattered throughout non-coding regions of human chromosomes.
* **CODIS 20 Core Loci:** The FBI Combined DNA Index System uses 20 core STR loci (e.g., D3S1358, vWA, FGA, TH01, TPOX, CSF1PO, D16S539, Amelogenin for sex determination) [Saferstein, Ch. 13; Li, Ch. 14].
* **Sex Determination (Amelogenin Locus):** Amelogenin gene on X chromosome is $106\text{ bp}$; Y chromosome is $112\text{ bp}$. Females ($XX$) yield 1 peak ($106\text{ bp}$); Males ($XY$) yield 2 peaks ($106, 112\text{ bp}$) [Saferstein, Ch. 13].

#### 4. Specialized Lineage Markers:
* **Y-STR Analysis:** Targets STR loci located strictly on the male-specific Y-chromosome (e.g., DYS391, DYS389). Invaluable for resolving male/female DNA mixtures in sexual assault cases where male DNA is present in low amounts [Saferstein, Ch. 13; Li, Ch. 15].
* **Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA):** Located in mitochondria outside the cell nucleus. Inherited strictly along the **maternal line**. Contains hypervariable regions (HV1, HV2). Used for analyzing degraded remains, hair shafts without roots, or ancient bones [Saferstein, Ch. 13; Li, Ch. 16].

### 5.3 Parentage Testing & Population Statistics

#### 1. Paternity Index (PI) Calculation:
Paternity testing compares the obligate paternal allele (OPA) shared between child and alleged father [Li, Ch. 17]:

$$\text{PI} = \frac{X}{Y}$$

* $X$ = Probability that the alleged father transmitted the obligate paternal allele (typically $0.5$ if heterozygous, $1.0$ if homozygous).
* $Y$ = Random match probability / frequency of the obligate allele in the general population ($p$).

#### 2. Combined Paternity Index (CPI) & Probability of Paternity ($W$):
Assuming independent locus inheritance (Product Rule):

$$\text{CPI} = \text{PI}_1 \cdot \text{PI}_2 \cdot \text{PI}_3 \dots \text{PI}_n$$

$$\text{Probability of Paternity } (W) = \left[ \frac{\text{CPI}}{\text{CPI} + 1} \right] \times 100\%$$

* (Assuming a neutral prior probability of $0.5$). A $W \ge 99.9\%$ establishes a legal inclusion [Li, Ch. 17].

#### 3. Hardy-Weinberg Principle & Random Match Probability (RMP):
In an infinite, randomly mating population, allele frequencies remain constant:

$$p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1$$

* **Homozygous Genotype Frequency:** $P = p^2$ (or $p^2 + p(1-p)\theta$ with subpopulation correction).
* **Heterozygous Genotype Frequency:** $P = 2pq$.
* **Overall Random Match Probability (Product Rule):** Multiply individual locus frequencies across all 20 independent STR loci:

$$\text{RMP} = P_{\text{locus 1}} \cdot P_{\text{locus 2}} \dots P_{\text{locus 20}}$$

```
                       Forensic Decision Matrix
                                   │
     ┌─────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┐
     ▼                             ▼                             ▼
  SEXUAL ASSAULT EVIDENCE       ARSON DEBRIS               HIT-AND-RUN BLOOD

  ├─ Differential Extraction    ├─ Passive Charcoal Strip  ├─ Takayama Confirmation
  └─ Y-STR Profiling            └─ GC-MS ASTM E1618        └─ STR DNA Profiling
```

### Decision Scenario 1: Sexual Assault Kit Analysis
* **Primary Objective:** Identify male seminal fluid in vaginal swab containing massive female epithelial cell background.
* **Selection Strategy:**
  1. *Screening:* Perform Brentamine Fast Blue Acid Phosphatase (AP) test on swab extract [Saferstein, Ch. 10].
  2. *Confirmation:* Microscopic identification of spermatozoa using Christmas Tree Stain or ABAcard p30 [Li, Ch. 5].
  3. *DNA Extraction:* Perform **Differential Extraction** using DTT to isolate male sperm DNA from female cell supernatant [Li, Ch. 11].
  4. *Profiling:* If female DNA overwhelms male DNA, execute **Y-STR Multiplex Amplification** [Saferstein, Ch. 13].

### Decision Scenario 2: Arson / Fire Debris Evidentiary Processing
* **Primary Objective:** Extract trace gasoline residues from scorched wooden flooring.
* **Selection Strategy:**
  1. Package wood immediately inside a sealed, airtight unlined paint can [Saferstein, Ch. 12].
  2. Insert an activated charcoal strip into the headspace; heat at $60^\circ\text{C}$ for 4 hours (Passive Headspace Concentration) [Evans-Nguyen, Ch. 3].
  3. Elute adsorbed hydrocarbons with carbon disulfide ($\text{CS}_2$).
  4. Inject extract into **GC-MS**; monitor target ion chromatograms ($m/z$ 91, 105, 119 for aromatics) according to ASTM E1618 [Saferstein, Ch. 12].

## Section 7: Practice Questions & Model Answers

### 7.1 Short-Answer Questions (3–5 Marks)

#### Question 1 (4 Marks):
*Differentiate between Low Explosives and High Explosives with respect to reaction mechanism, velocity, and two forensic examples of each.*

##### Model Answer:
1. **Reaction Mechanism:** Low explosives undergo **deflagration** (a rapid subsonic chemical combustion process requiring confinement to explode), whereas high explosives undergo **detonation** (an instantaneous supersonic shock-wave reaction) [Saferstein, Ch. 12].
2. **Reaction Velocity:** Low explosives propagate at speeds $<1000\text{ m/s}$; high explosives propagate at supersonic velocities ranging from $1000\text{ m/s}$ to $9000\text{ m/s}$ [Saferstein, Ch. 12].
3. **Examples:**
   * *Low Explosives:* Black powder (potassium nitrate, sulfur, charcoal) and Smokeless powder (nitrocellulose) [Saferstein, Ch. 12].
   * *High Explosives:* RDX (Cyclonite) and TNT (Trinitrotoluene) [Saferstein, Ch. 12].

#### Question 2 (5 Marks):
*Explain the principle, chemical reaction, and microscopic output of the Takayama test for blood confirmation.*

##### Model Answer:
1. **Principle:** The Takayama test (hemochromogen crystal test) is a definitive confirmatory test for blood that identifies the heme moiety of hemoglobin [Saferstein, Ch. 10; Li, Ch. 3].
2. **Chemical Reaction:** Blood extract is treated with Takayama reagent (containing pyridine, sodium hydroxide, glucose, and water) and gently heated. Alkaline oxidation-reduction by glucose converts hemoglobin into reduced heme, which coordinates with pyridine [Li, Ch. 3].
3. **Microscopic Output:** Under light microscopy ($400\times$), the reaction produces distinctive **pink, feathery, needle-like crystals of pyridine hemochromogen** [Saferstein, Ch. 10; Biswas, Ch. 5].

##### Model Answer:

## Section 8: Formula Sheet & Worked Numerical Examples

### Formula Summary
1. **Angle of Impact:** $\theta = \arcsin\left(\frac{\text{Width } W}{\text{Length } L}\right)$
2. **Area of Origin Height:** $H = D \cdot \tan(\theta)$
3. **Widmark BAC Formula:** $\text{BAC} = \left[\frac{A}{P \cdot r}\right] - (\beta \cdot t)$
4. **Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium:** $p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1$
5. **Combined Paternity Index:** $\text{CPI} = \prod_{i=1}^{n} \text{PI}_i$
6. **Probability of Paternity ($W$):** $W = \left[\frac{\text{CPI}}{\text{CPI} + 1}\right] \times 100\%$

---

### Worked Numerical Example 1: BPA Angle of Impact & Origin Height

#### Problem Inputs:
A bloodstain on a vertical wall measures Width $W = 7.5\text{ mm}$ and Length $L = 15.0\text{ mm}$. The distance from this stain to the 2D Area of Convergence on the floor is $D = 2.10\text{ meters}$. Calculate:
1. The Angle of Impact ($\theta$).
2. The 3D Height of Origin ($H$) above the floor.

#### Step-by-Step Solution:
1. **Calculate Angle of Impact ($\theta$):**
   $$\frac{W}{L} = \frac{7.5\text{ mm}}{15.0\text{ mm}} = 0.500$$
   $$\theta = \arcsin(0.500) = 30.0^\circ$$

2. **Calculate 3D Height of Origin ($H$):**
   $$H = D \cdot \tan(\theta) = 2.10\text{ m} \cdot \tan(30.0^\circ)$$
   $$\tan(30.0^\circ) = 0.57735$$
   $$H = 2.10\text{ m} \cdot 0.57735 = 1.212\text{ meters}$$

#### Final Result:
* Angle of Impact ($\theta$) = **$30.0^\circ$**
* Area of Origin Height ($H$) = **$1.21\text{ meters}$ ($121.2\text{ cm}$)** above the floor convergence line.

---

### Worked Numerical Example 2: Widmark Blood Alcohol Concentration (BAC)

#### Problem Inputs:
A male subject weighing $P = 75\text{ kg}$ consumes an alcoholic beverage containing $A = 45\text{ grams}$ of pure ethanol. Assume male distribution factor $r = 0.68$ and hourly elimination rate $\beta = 0.015\% \text{ w/v per hour}$ ($0.15\text{ g/L/hr}$). Calculate his BAC after $t = 3.0\text{ hours}$.

#### Step-by-Step Solution:
1. **Calculate Peak BAC before elimination:**
   $$\text{Peak BAC} = \frac{A}{P \cdot r} = \frac{45\text{ g}}{75\text{ kg} \cdot 0.68\text{ L/kg}} = \frac{45}{51.0\text{ L}} = 0.88235\text{ g/L} = 0.0882\%\text{ w/v}$$

2. **Calculate Metabolism Elimination over 3 Hours:**
   $$\text{Elimination} = \beta \cdot t = 0.015\% \cdot 3.0 = 0.045\%\text{ w/v}$$

3. **Calculate Net BAC at $t = 3.0\text{ hours}$:**
   $$\text{BAC} = 0.0882\% - 0.0450\% = 0.0432\%\text{ w/v } (0.432\text{ g/L})$$

#### Final Result:
* Blood Alcohol Concentration at 3 hours = **$0.043\%\text{ w/v}$ ($0.43\text{ g/L}$ or $43.2\text{ mg/dL}$)**.

---

### Worked Numerical Example 3: Parentage Testing Probability ($W$)

#### Problem Inputs:
In a disputed paternity case across 15 STR loci, the product of individual Paternity Indexes yields a Combined Paternity Index $\text{CPI} = 2500$. Calculate the Probability of Paternity ($W$) assuming a neutral prior probability ($0.5$).

#### Step-by-Step Solution:
$$W = \left[ \frac{\text{CPI}}{\text{CPI} + 1} \right] \times 100\% = \left[ \frac{2500}{2500 + 1} \right] \times 100\% = \left[ \frac{2500}{2501} \right] \times 100\%$$
$$W = 0.99960015 \times 100\% = 99.960\%$$

#### Final Result:
* Probability of Paternity ($W$) = **$99.96\%$** (Establishes legal inclusion of alleged father).

## Section 9: Glossary of Technical Terms

* **Accelerant:** Any flammable liquid, solid, or gas used to initiate or speed the spread of a fire.
* **Acid Phosphatase (AP):** An enzyme secreted in high concentrations by the human prostate gland into seminal plasma; key marker in presumptive semen testing.
* **Allelic Ladder:** A reference standard containing known DNA fragment sizes for precise STR allele calling in capillary electrophoresis.
* **Antigen:** A macromolecule (protein or carbohydrate) capable of inducing an immune response and binding specifically to an antibody.
* **Arterial Gushing:** Blood exit pattern resulting from trauma to a major artery under systemic pressure.
* **Brisance:** The shattering capability of a high explosive, directly proportional to detonation velocity.
* **Cast-Off Pattern:** Bloodstain pattern created when blood is released from a moving blood-bearing object.
* **CODIS:** Combined DNA Index System operated by the FBI, indexing 20 core STR loci across offender and crime scene profiles.
* **Deflagration:** Rapid chemical combustion where the reaction front advances at subsonic speed ($<1000\text{ m/s}$).
* **Detonation:** Instantaneous chemical explosion propagating at supersonic shock wave speed ($1000–9000\text{ m/s}$).
* **Differential Extraction:** Isolation method separating male sperm DNA from female epithelial DNA in sexual assault evidence.
* **Luminol:** Chemiluminescent reagent ($3$-aminophthalhydrazide) that emits blue light when oxidized by heme iron in darkness.
* **Passive Droplet:** A blood drop formed solely under the influence of gravity.
* **PMR (Postmortem Redistribution):** Changes in drug concentrations in blood after death due to tissue diffusion.
* **Secretor:** An individual ($80\%$ of population) who secretes soluble ABO blood group antigens into body fluids (saliva, semen).
* **Short Tandem Repeat (STR):** Short repeating DNA sequence motif ($2-6\text{ bp}$) used for forensic DNA profiling.
* **Takayama Test:** Confirmatory crystal test for blood yielding pink feathery pyridine hemochromogen crystals.
* **Widmark Factor ($r$):** Fraction representing the body volume into which alcohol is distributed ($0.68$ for males, $0.55$ for females).
* **Y-STR:** Short tandem repeat loci located exclusively on the male Y-chromosome.

### 10.1 Presumptive vs. Confirmatory Body Fluid Assay Cheat Sheet

| Body Fluid | Presumptive Test | Presumptive Output | Confirmatory Test | Confirmatory Output |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Blood** | Kastle-Meyer (KM) | Pink Color | Takayama Test | Pink Feathery Hemochromogen Crystals |
| **Semen** | Brentamine Fast Blue | Purple Color ($<30\text{ sec}$) | Christmas Tree Stain / ABAcard p30 | Red Heads/Green Tails Sperm / p30 Pink Line |
| **Saliva** | Phadebas / Starch-Iodine | Blue Soluble Dye / Loss of Blue | SALIgAE / Human Salivary Amylase | Specific Antigen Reaction Line |
| **Sweat** | Silver Nitrate ($\text{AgNO}_3$) | White $\text{AgCl} \to$ Dark UV | Dermcidin ELISA | Specific Protein Binding |
| **Urine** | Nessler's / Jaffé Test | Yellow-Brown / Red Picrate | Tamm-Horsfall Protein Assay | Specific Uromodulin Line |

### 10.2 Velocity vs. Spatter Drop Characteristic Cheat Sheet

| Velocity Class | Droplet Impact Velocity | Resulting Stain Diameter | Typical Forensic Origin |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Low Velocity** | $<1.5\text{ m/s}$ ($<5\text{ ft/s}$) | $>4\text{ mm}$ | Passive dripping, free fall, stepping in blood. |
| **Medium Velocity** | $1.5-7.5\text{ m/s}$ ($5-25\text{ ft/s}$) | $1-4\text{ mm}$ | Blunt force trauma, stabbing, fist beating. |
| **High Velocity** | $\ge 30\text{ m/s}$ ($\ge 100\text{ ft/s}$) | $<1\text{ mm}$ (Fine mist) | Gunshot trauma, high-speed machinery, explosives. |
